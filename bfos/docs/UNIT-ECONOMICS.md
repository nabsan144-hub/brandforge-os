# Unit economics — a measurement plan, not a profit claim

Keep the price points; test whether the product earns them. The former unlimited Cloud plan is replaced by a 300-campaign allowance.

| Cloud plan | Listed revenue | Allowance | Gross revenue/run at full monthly allowance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pro monthly | $49/month | 50 / UTC calendar month | $0.98 |
| Agency | $99/month | 300 / UTC calendar month | $0.33 |
| Pro annual | $490/year charged upfront | Same 50 / calendar month | Depends on active calendar windows; do not assume 600 exactly at partial-year boundaries |

These are **not margins**. Subtract actual payment/FX/refund/chargeback costs, model tokens, hosting/database/egress, email/messaging, support time, acquisition and overhead. Desktop $199/$499 revenue also has support, maintenance, refunds and acquisition costs even when generation runs locally.

The operator budget guard reserves a conservative per-run upper bound from declared provider rates. It prevents uncontrolled operator spend, but is not an invoice or a measurement of customer willingness to pay. Reported token counts can include personal-key traffic and incomplete provider reporting; do not call them audited operator spend.

For a pilot, record per cohort: activated users, reviewed/exported packs, time-to-usable-output, support minutes, paid conversion, retained users, refunds, actual invoices and contribution after variable costs. Use the optional feedback form, private generation records and provider/Paddle reports. Do not publish invented customer counts, ROI or testimonials.

Increase limits only after measuring output usefulness and cost. If customers reject the displayed price, improve the reviewed deliverable and handoff workflow or adjust the offer before spending on acquisition. Do not solve retention with an unbudgeted unlimited promise.
